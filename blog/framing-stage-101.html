Framing Stage 101: What's Happening During the Most Dramatic Phase of Your Build

It's the phase clients love watching most, because a house suddenly appears in a matter of weeks. Here's what's actually happening.

B09Feature image · 16:9

If you ask clients which phase of construction they found most exciting to watch, framing wins almost every time. It's the moment a foundation becomes a recognizable house, often in a matter of weeks. Here's what's actually happening on site during this stage.

If you've never watched a house get framed in person, it's worth a site visit purely for how much it changes your sense of scale, compared to standing on a foundation slab or looking at a floor plan on paper.

Walls go up first, and fast

Exterior and load-bearing interior walls are typically framed on the ground, then lifted into place, sometimes with a crane on larger or more complex builds. This is why framing can look like it's moving impossibly fast compared to earlier site work: most of the labor-intensive assembly already happened before the wall was ever standing.

Building walls flat on the deck before standing them also lets the crew work more precisely, checking every measurement while the wall is easy to access, rather than trying to correct anything after it's already vertical and braced.

The roof structure is where the real engineering shows

Once walls are set and braced, trusses or rafters go in to form the roof structure. This is often the most visually dramatic part of the whole build, crews working at height, a crane lifting truss sections into place, and the home's final roofline becoming visible for the first time.

Sheathing comes next, and it's more than plywood

Once the frame is up, structural sheathing goes over the walls and roof. We use ZIP System sheathing specifically because it doubles as part of the home's air and water barrier, taped at every seam as it goes on, rather than treating the weather barrier as a separate step later.

This is also the point where window and door rough openings get their first flashing details, well before the windows themselves arrive on site, because getting this sequence right is what keeps water out of the wall assembly for the life of the home.

Why this phase moves faster than people expect

Framing crews work in a tight, practiced sequence, and most of a home's framing (walls, roof structure, and sheathing) can happen in just a few weeks once the foundation is ready. It's fast because it's one of the most standardized phases of construction, not because anything is being rushed.

What we're actually checking during this phase

  • That framing matches the engineered plans exactly, especially load-bearing points
  • That sheathing seams are properly taped before the next trade covers them
  • That window and door openings are correctly sized and flashed before siding goes on
  • That the structure is square, plumb, and ready for the mechanical trades that follow

It's also the phase where the true scale and layout of a home becomes real for clients, often for the first time. Walking a framed house, before drywall hides the structure, tells you more about how a home will actually feel than any floor plan on paper.

We inspect framing before it gets covered by anything else, because it's the last point where structural issues are easy and inexpensive to correct. Once insulation and drywall go up, the same fix costs far more and takes far longer.

Weather and how it affects the schedule

Framing crews can work through a fair amount of weather, but sustained rain or high wind will pause work, particularly once crews are up at height on a roof structure. We build weather contingency into every framing schedule rather than promise a specific date that a rainy week could immediately blow past.

What matters more than avoiding every weather delay is protecting the structure once it's up. A framed home that gets rained on is not a problem by itself, wood dries out, but a framed home that sits open to weather for too long without sheathing and a dried-in roof is worth asking your builder about directly.
