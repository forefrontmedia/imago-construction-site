What to Expect: A Custom Home Timeline, Start to Move-In

A realistic look at how long each phase takes, so you can plan your move (and your patience) accordingly.

B03Feature image · 16:9

"How long will this take?" is the second question every client asks us, right after "what will this cost?" The honest answer is that it depends on the home, the site, and how prepared you are before we break ground, but the phases themselves are predictable. Here's what actually happens, and roughly how long each step takes.

We share this same breakdown with every client during the first consultation, because a realistic schedule set early is far more useful than an optimistic one that shifts every month.

Phase 1: Consultation and planning (4-10 weeks)

This is where we meet at your land or your current home, talk through what you want, and either help shape a floor plan or start building from plans you already have. This phase moves as fast as decisions get made. Clients who come in with a clear plan and a firm budget move through this stage in a few weeks; clients still exploring options take longer, which is completely fine, but worth planning around.

This is also the phase where budget gets locked in, or at least gets close. We'd rather spend an extra two weeks here refining a plan against your actual budget than start construction on a design that has to get value-engineered down once framing is already underway. It's a far cheaper place to make changes than after the foundation is poured.

Phase 2: Permitting and site prep (2-6 weeks)

Once plans are finalized, permits get submitted and site work begins: clearing, grading, and utility work if the lot needs it. Timeline here depends heavily on your specific county and lot conditions.

Phase 3: Foundation and framing (4-8 weeks)

This is the phase where progress finally becomes visible, and it's usually the most exciting part for clients to watch. Foundation work goes in first, then the frame goes up, walls, roof structure, and sheathing, in a matter of weeks. This is also the stage where the true scale of the home becomes obvious for the first time.

Phase 4: Mechanicals, insulation, and drywall (6-10 weeks)

Once the home is framed and weathertight, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC get roughed in, followed by insulation and air sealing, then drywall. This is a slower, less visually dramatic phase, but it's where the high-performance details (the ones you can't see later) get built in correctly or not at all.

This phase is also when you'll want to finalize any low-voltage or smart-home wiring, security cameras, structured audio, extra network drops, since running that wiring after drywall is up means cutting into finished walls. We flag this decision point specifically so it doesn't get missed in the excitement of watching framing happen.

Phase 5: Finishes (8-14 weeks)

Flooring, cabinetry, countertops, trim, paint, tile, and fixtures. This is the longest single phase and the one where your specific selections most affect the schedule; custom or special-order materials can add lead time here.

Phase 6: Final walkthrough and move-in (2-3 weeks)

Punch-list items get resolved, final inspections happen, and we walk the home with you room by room before handing over the keys.

Total: typically 8-14 months

Most custom homes we build land somewhere in that range, start to finish, depending on size, site complexity, and how far in advance selections are made. We give every client a project-specific schedule once plans are finalized, and we keep you updated at every phase, not just when something changes.

Weather, material lead times, and how quickly selections get finalized are the three biggest variables that push a project toward the longer end of that range. We build a realistic schedule buffer into every project plan rather than promise an optimistic date and miss it, because a builder who's honest about timeline up front is a lot easier to work with than one who isn't.

What can push a timeline longer

Weather is the most obvious variable, a wet spring can delay site work and foundation pours by weeks, and there's not much any builder can do about it beyond building buffer into the schedule from the start. Material lead times are the second biggest factor, particularly on custom or special-order items like certain windows, cabinetry, or specialty tile, which can take months to arrive depending on the manufacturer.

The variable most within your control is decision speed on selections. A client who finalizes flooring, cabinetry, and fixture choices early keeps the finish phase moving; a client still deciding once cabinets need to be ordered can add real weeks to the schedule waiting on a choice rather than waiting on a trade. We build in decision deadlines during the planning phase specifically to protect your own move-in date.
