Choosing the Right Lot: What to Look for Before You Buy Land to Build On

The lot you choose shapes your budget and your finished home more than almost any other decision. Here's what to check before you buy.

B05Feature image · 16:9

We get called about a lot of land after it's already been purchased, and occasionally that's when we have to deliver bad news about cost or buildability. The best time to involve a builder is before you close, not after. Here's what we look for.

This isn't a comprehensive engineering checklist, it's the practical version, the same questions we ask on an actual lot walk with clients, before either of us has spent real money.

Access and utilities

Is there existing road access, or does a driveway need to be cut? Is public water and sewer available, or will the home need a well and septic system, which requires a soil (perc) test before you can even confirm the lot is buildable? These questions can eliminate a lot before you fall in love with the view.

Rural East Tennessee lots in particular can look move-in ready and still require a well, a septic system, and a long driveway cut through wooded terrain, all of which are real costs that belong in your budget from the very first conversation, not discovered after you own the land.

Topography and soil

A sloped or wooded lot (common, and often beautiful, in East Tennessee) can require significant grading, retaining walls, or engineered foundations. That's not a reason to walk away, but it is a cost that needs to be part of your budget from day one, not a surprise after closing.

Some of the most desirable lots in our area, the ones with views, privacy, and mature trees, are also the ones with the most site-work complexity. That's not a reason to avoid them; it's a reason to know the real number before you commit, so the view doesn't come with a budget surprise attached.

Sun orientation and views

Where the sun rises and sets relative to your main living spaces affects natural light, energy use, and how the home actually feels to live in. A lot with a great view in the wrong direction can still work, it just needs a floor plan designed around it rather than against it.

Setbacks, easements, and restrictions

Every lot has rules: setback lines, utility easements, floodplain restrictions, and sometimes HOA or covenant requirements. These determine your actual buildable envelope, which can be meaningfully smaller than the total lot size suggests.

Flood risk and drainage

Especially relevant near our lakes and rivers: check flood zone maps and ask how water moves across the lot during heavy rain. Good drainage design during site work prevents expensive problems later.

We've walked lots that looked perfectly dry on a summer afternoon but sit in a documented floodway once you pull the FEMA maps. A five-minute records check can save you from a lot you can't insure affordably, or can't build on at all.

What we do before you close

  • Walk the lot with you in person, not just look at a listing photo
  • Flag anything that will add meaningful site-work cost
  • Give you a rough sense of where a home would sit and how it would orient
  • Point you toward the right soil test or survey if one hasn't been done

A lot walk before you buy usually takes an hour and can save months of frustration and thousands of dollars in surprises. If you're seriously considering a piece of land anywhere in Knox, Loudon, Blount, Anderson, Roane, or Monroe County, we're happy to meet you there.

Working with an agent who understands new construction

Not every real estate agent regularly works with buyers who plan to build. An agent experienced with raw land and building lots will already know to pull soil test history, flood maps, and utility availability before you ever make an offer, which can save real time compared to finding these issues out afterward.

If your agent hasn't handled a land purchase built for new construction before, that's not a dealbreaker, but it's a good reason to loop in your builder earlier in the search rather than after you've found "the one." We're always glad to jump on a call with your agent directly.
