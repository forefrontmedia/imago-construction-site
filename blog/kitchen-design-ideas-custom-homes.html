Kitchen Design Ideas We're Seeing in East Tennessee Custom Homes

A look at the layouts, materials, and details our clients are actually choosing right now, not just what's trending online.

B06Feature image · 16:9

Kitchens are where clients spend the most time in the design process, and for good reason: it's the room a family actually lives in. Here's what we're seeing requested most often in recent East Tennessee custom builds, and why these choices tend to hold up over time.

These aren't pulled from a design magazine; they're patterns we're seeing repeat across real projects with real families, which is a better source of truth than whatever's trending online this month.

Larger islands, built for more than prep space

Islands have grown from a prep surface into the functional center of the kitchen: seating for casual meals, a second sink, extra storage, and sometimes the main gathering spot during parties. We're designing islands large enough to genuinely multi-task, rather than a small accent piece.

We're also seeing more islands designed with mixed-height counters, a slightly raised bar section for seating separated from a lower prep section, so the space works equally well for a quick breakfast and for serious cooking, without one function getting in the way of the other.

Walk-in pantries over pantry cabinets

Given the choice, most clients now prefer a small walk-in pantry over an equivalent run of pantry cabinetry. It's not necessarily more storage, but it keeps countertop clutter (small appliances, bulk goods) out of the main kitchen entirely.

A dedicated coffee or beverage station

A small secondary counter area with its own outlets and cabinet storage, separate from the main workflow, is one of the most-requested small upgrades we're seeing, and one of the least expensive relative to how much daily use it gets.

These stations are also where we're seeing the most requests for a small second dishwasher drawer or an under-counter beverage fridge, small additions that keep everyday traffic out of the main kitchen workflow entirely.

Mixed cabinet finishes

Rather than one uniform cabinet color throughout, we're building more kitchens with a contrasting island (a darker or bolder tone against lighter perimeter cabinetry), which adds visual interest without committing to a bold color on every surface.

Working pantries and prep zones near the garage or mudroom

For clients who cook and entertain frequently, we're seeing more requests for a secondary prep or storage zone positioned near the garage entry, so groceries and event supplies don't have to cross the whole kitchen.

Practical lighting layers, not just statement fixtures

Pendant lighting over the island still matters for style, but we're spending more design time on task lighting (under-cabinet, over the range) so the kitchen actually functions well at night, not just photographs well in daylight.

What actually matters most

Trends aside, the kitchens that work best years later are the ones designed around how a specific family actually cooks and gathers, not a showroom layout. That's the conversation we start with before we talk finishes at all.

We always ask clients to walk us through how they actually cook a typical weeknight dinner and a typical holiday gathering, because those two very different scenarios usually reveal the real design priorities better than any list of finishes ever could.

Where to spend, and where to save

If budget requires trade-offs, we generally steer clients toward spending on the things that are expensive or disruptive to change later, cabinetry layout, plumbing and electrical placement, ventilation, and saving on things that are easy to upgrade down the road, like hardware, light fixtures, or even countertops in some cases.

Appliances are a category worth deciding early rather than late, since cabinetry openings are often sized around specific appliance dimensions. Choosing a range or refrigerator after cabinets are ordered is one of the more common and avoidable causes of a kitchen delay.

Bring us your Pinterest board (or your list of pet peeves from your current kitchen) and we'll help shape a layout that works.
