Universal Design Features Worth Adding to Your Forever Home

Features that make a home easier to live in at every stage of life, without making it feel like a medical facility.

B11Feature image · 16:9

More of our clients are asking about "aging in place" or "universal design" features, not because they need them today, but because they're building a home they intend to stay in for decades. Done well, these features are invisible: they simply make a home easier to live in at every age, without looking clinical.

None of this requires designing a home that looks or feels different from any other custom build in the neighborhood. It just requires deciding on these details early, while they're still cheap and easy to include.

A no-step entry, at least at one door

A single ground-level entry, even if the rest of the home has steps, means the house remains fully accessible without a major renovation later. It's a simple grading and design decision at the time of building, and a genuinely difficult retrofit afterward.

This is easiest to plan for during initial site grading, when the elevation of the home relative to the driveway or a side entry is still being decided, and essentially impossible to retrofit gracefully once the foundation and grading are finished.

Wider hallways and doorways

Building hallways and door openings slightly wider than the strict minimum costs very little at the framing stage and makes a real difference for mobility, moving furniture, and simple day-to-day comfort, whether or not accessibility is ever a factor.

A primary suite on the main level

Even in a two-story home, placing the primary suite on the main floor means the most essential living spaces, bedroom, bathroom, and often a home office, never require using stairs. This is consistently one of the most requested "future-proofing" features we build.

Curbless showers

A curbless, walk-in shower removes a tripping hazard, looks clean and modern, and requires no retrofit later. It's become one of the more popular bathroom choices among clients of every age, not just those planning ahead for mobility.

Beyond accessibility, curbless showers have simply become a popular, clean design choice on their own merits, which means these features blend into the home's overall style rather than standing out as a specialty accommodation.

Lever handles and accessible switch heights

Lever-style door handles and slightly lower light switch placement are small, inexpensive specification choices at the time of building, easier to use for everyone, and genuinely helpful for anyone with limited grip strength or mobility, at any age.

Blocking in the walls, even if you don't need grab bars yet

Adding solid wood blocking behind bathroom walls during framing means grab bars can be installed later, anytime, without opening up finished walls. It costs almost nothing during construction and removes a real barrier to adding support later if it's ever needed.

We include this on essentially every primary bathroom we build now, because the cost is negligible during framing and the option it preserves, real support installed cleanly, years down the road, without ever opening a finished wall, is exactly the kind of quiet planning a forever home deserves.

None of these features need to announce themselves. Built thoughtfully, they simply make a home more comfortable now and far more adaptable later, which is exactly what a forever home should do.

Features that help right now, not just later

Almost everything on this list benefits a household of any age, not just aging homeowners planning decades ahead. Wider hallways make moving furniture easier today. A no-step entry is simpler for carrying groceries or a stroller. Lever handles are easier to use with an armful of laundry, at any age.

That's really the point of universal design done well: it's not a set of accommodations bolted onto a home, it's just good, thoughtful design that happens to keep working for you at every stage of life, without ever needing to be labeled as anything special.
